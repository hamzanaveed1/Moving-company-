"How much will it cost?" is the first question almost everyone asks, and the honest answer is: it depends on a handful of things you can mostly control. This guide explains what actually moves the price of a UK removal, gives realistic ranges to budget against, and shows where people get caught out.

> **Please note:** the figures below are indicative market ranges to help you plan, not a quote. Your price depends on your volume, your access and your date. A reputable company will give you a **written fixed price** once they know the details.

## What decides the price of a removal

Removal companies are really selling **time, people and vehicle space**. Everything else is a variation on that.

- **Volume of belongings.** More furniture and boxes means a bigger van, more crew or more trips. This is usually the single biggest factor.
- **Distance.** Local moves are mostly crew time. Long-distance moves add fuel, driver hours, tolls and sometimes an overnight stop.
- **Access at both ends.** Stairs with no lift, a long carry from the van, narrow streets and parking restrictions all add time. A third-floor walk-up can cost noticeably more than a ground-floor house of the same size.
- **Date.** Fridays, month-end, bank holidays and school summer holidays are the busiest. Midweek dates are often easier to schedule and sometimes cheaper.
- **Services included.** Packing, dismantling and rebuilding furniture, storage and unpacking are all extras on top of the basic move.

## Typical UK removal costs by property size

These ranges assume a standard full-service crew, a normal level of access and no packing. They are broad on purpose, because real prices vary by region and season.

| Property size | Local move (same city, under ~20 miles) | Long distance (~200 miles) |
|---|---|---|
| Studio / 1 bedroom flat | £250 – £550 | £600 – £1,100 |
| 2 bedroom home | £400 – £800 | £850 – £1,500 |
| 3 bedroom home | £600 – £1,200 | £1,200 – £2,200 |
| 4 bedroom home | £850 – £1,700 | £1,600 – £3,000 |
| 5+ bedroom home | £1,200 – £2,400+ | £2,200 – £4,000+ |

London and the South East are usually at the upper end, while some northern cities and Wales are often lower. Shared-load options on long-distance routes can cost less if you can be flexible on dates.

## Hourly rate or fixed price?

**Hourly pricing** suits small, simple local moves with easy access. You pay for the time on the clock, so a well-prepared move is cheaper. The risk is that delays, such as slow lifts, a late key handover or poor parking, add to the bill.

**Fixed pricing** suits bigger or more complex moves, and nearly all long-distance moves. You agree the total up front. The company takes on the risk of the job running long, which is why a good quote asks detailed questions first.

Ask any company what is included in a fixed price: crew size, hours, fuel, tolls, insurance, wrapping materials and dismantling.

## Extras that add to the cost

| Extra | What to expect |
|---|---|
| Full packing service | Often a few hundred pounds for a flat and more for a large house, plus materials |
| Packing materials only | Boxes, tape and paper are usually charged by the item or as a pack |
| Dismantle and rebuild furniture | Often included for standard beds and wardrobes. Complex items can be extra |
| Storage | Usually charged weekly or monthly, with collection and delivery on top |
| Piano or very heavy items | Specialist handling is normally an add-on |
| Extra crew for stairs | Worth it for tenements and high walk-ups, and safer for your belongings |

## The costs people forget

The removal quote is rarely the whole bill. Budget for these as well:

1. **Parking suspension or permit.** In many busy streets you need to apply to the council for a temporary parking bay suspension. Fees and notice periods vary by council, so check early.
2. **Emissions and congestion charges.** London's ULEZ and Congestion Charge, Birmingham, Bristol, Glasgow and Edinburgh's low-emission zones can apply to the vehicle. Ask whether any charge is in your quote.
3. **Tolls and crossings.** Examples include the Mersey tunnels and the Tyne Tunnel.
4. **Cleaning and key handovers.** End-of-tenancy cleaning, and keeping cash or flexibility for a late completion.
5. **Mail redirection.** Royal Mail's redirection service is a paid service, usually for a set number of months.
6. **Storage if completion dates slip.** It is cheaper to plan this than to arrange it in a hurry.

## How to get an accurate quote

1. **List everything**, including loft, garage, shed and garden items. Under-declaring volume is the main reason prices rise on the day.
2. **Describe access honestly:** floors, lifts, steps, distance from the parking spot to the door and any narrow turns.
3. **Ask for a video or in-person survey** for bigger moves. It is the best protection against a surprise.
4. **Get three written quotes** and compare like with like: crew size, hours, insurance and what is excluded.
5. **Confirm the deposit and cancellation terms** in writing.

## Seven honest ways to save money

- **Declutter first.** Every box you do not move is money saved. Sell, donate or recycle before you pack.
- **Be flexible on the date.** A midweek move is often easier to fit in than a Friday.
- **Pack it yourself.** Packing is time-consuming for a crew, so doing it yourself reduces cost. Our [packing guide](/blog/how-to-pack-for-a-house-move/) shows how to do it safely.
- **Dismantle simple furniture in advance** if you are comfortable doing so.
- **Make access easy.** Arrange parking close to the door and book any lifts in advance.
- **Consider a smaller option** for small loads. See [man and van vs removal company](/blog/man-and-van-vs-removal-company/).
- **Compare shared and dedicated loads** for long-distance moves.

## Warning signs of a quote that is too good to be true

Be cautious if a company quotes by phone without asking about your inventory or access, asks for a large cash deposit, has no verifiable address or company details, or cannot show its goods-in-transit insurance.
