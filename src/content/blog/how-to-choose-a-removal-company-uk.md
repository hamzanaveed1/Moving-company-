You are handing a stranger the contents of your home. Choosing carefully matters, and most of the checks take less than an hour. Here is what to look for, what to ask, and the warning signs that should make you walk away.

## 1. Get at least three written quotes

Quotes let you compare like with like. Ask each company to put in writing:

- The **total price** and what it includes
- The **date, arrival window and crew size**
- The **vehicle size** and number of trips
- Whether **fuel, tolls, parking and stairs** are included
- **Packing, dismantling and rebuilding** if you need them
- The **insurance cover** and excess
- **Deposit and cancellation terms**

A company that asks detailed questions before quoting is usually a good sign. A flat price given in two minutes with no questions is not.

## 2. Ask for a survey for bigger moves

For a three-bedroom home or bigger, or a tricky property, a **video or in-person survey** helps the company see what is moving, spot access problems and give an accurate price. It also protects you from "the van is too small" surprises on the day.

## 3. Check insurance properly

Do not accept "yes, we are insured" as an answer. Ask:

- Do you have **goods-in-transit insurance**? What is the cover limit and the excess?
- Do you have **public liability insurance**?
- Is the vehicle covered for **hire and reward**?
- What is **excluded**, such as high-value items, items you pack yourself, or items in storage?

Ask for the policy summary or certificate and check the dates. Also check **your own home contents insurance**, as some policies cover items in transit and some do not.

## 4. Look for trade body membership, and verify it

Membership of a trade body, such as the British Association of Removers (BAR), can indicate a company has met standards and offers a complaints process. But anyone can claim membership, so **check the member directory on the trade body's own website** instead of relying on a logo.

## 5. Read reviews critically

- Look across **multiple sources**, not just one site.
- Read the **recent** reviews and the **negative ones**. How the company responds tells you a lot.
- Be cautious of **dozens of near-identical five-star reviews** posted in a short space of time.
- Look for **specific detail**, such as the crew's names, the route or the property.

Ask for recent **references** from customers with similar moves, especially for long-distance or high-value jobs.

## 6. Check they are a real, traceable business

A reputable company will give you:

- A **registered business name** and **company number** (check it at Companies House)
- A **physical address**, not just a mobile number
- A **landline or business number** and a professional email address
- Clear **terms and conditions** in writing

## 7. Understand the contract and cancellation terms

Read the terms before paying a deposit. Look at:

- What happens if **you** need to change the date
- What happens if **they** need to change the date, and what compensation applies
- How to **make a claim** for damage, and how long you have to report it
- Any **liability limits** per item or per move

Note down any damage on the day, before the crew leaves, and photograph it. Report it in writing as soon as possible.

## 8. Be wary of big deposits and cash-only deals

A small deposit to secure a date is normal. Be cautious if:

- A company asks for **a large payment upfront** or the **full amount before the move**
- They only accept **cash** and will not provide a receipt
- They pressure you to **book immediately** or pay to "hold the price"

Pay by card where possible, as it can give you additional protection if something goes wrong.

## Red flags at a glance

| Red flag | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Quote given without asking about your inventory or access | Price is likely to change on the day |
| No physical address or company number | Hard to trace if something goes wrong |
| Refuses to show insurance | May have none, or not for hire and reward |
| Vague terms such as "approximately" for everything | Leaves room for extra charges |
| Large cash deposit requested | High risk |
| Vehicle or crew details are not confirmed | You may get a smaller van than needed |
| Only five-star reviews with generic comments | May not be genuine |

## Questions to ask on the phone

1. How many people and what vehicle will you send?
2. Is the price fixed? What could make it change?
3. What insurance do you have, and can I see the policy?
4. How do you handle delays and completion-day changes?
5. Who will be my contact on the day?
6. What is your process if something is damaged?

## What a good quote looks like

A good quote reads like a plan. It names the date and times, the crew and vehicle, what is included, what is not, the insurance and the terms. It makes you feel the company has thought about your move before they have touched a box.
