Moving 100 miles or more is a different job from moving across town. There is more to coordinate, more that can slip, and less margin for error. These twelve tips come from the planning that goes into a typical city-to-city move, whether it is London to Manchester, Leeds to Newcastle or Birmingham to Bristol.

## 1. Start earlier than you think

Book four to six weeks ahead if you can, and earlier for busy periods such as summer, month-end and the weeks before term. Long-distance vehicles and crews are scheduled in advance, so late bookings have fewer options.

## 2. Understand dedicated vs shared loads

- A **dedicated load** means a vehicle carries only your belongings and travels directly. It is the most flexible on dates and timing.
- A **shared load** combines several customers on one route. It is usually cheaper, but you get a delivery window rather than a fixed time.

If your completion date is firm, choose dedicated. If you can be flexible, ask for both prices.

## 3. Plan around legal driver hours

Commercial drivers are limited in how long they can drive and must take breaks. On routes like [London to Edinburgh](/routes/london-to-edinburgh-removals/), around 400 miles, the journey is usually too long for a single day once loading and unloading are included. A good company will tell you this honestly and plan an overnight stop. Routes of 100 to 250 miles, such as [London to Birmingham](/routes/london-to-birmingham-removals/) or [Manchester to Leeds](/routes/manchester-to-leeds-removals/), are usually same-day.

## 4. Build a buffer into completion day

The biggest source of stress is a delivery that depends on a chain completing. Ask your solicitor or letting agent for realistic key-release times, and avoid planning a delivery for the minute you expect the keys. Some people deliberately load on one day and deliver on the next. If your dates do not line up, **storage-in-transit** gives you a safety net. See our [storage service](/services/storage/).

## 5. Check access at the destination, not just where you are leaving

A van that works at your current street may not at the new one. Check:

- Parking and loading restrictions
- Steps, stairs and lifts
- Low bridges, narrow roads and weight restrictions
- Whether you need a council parking suspension

Send photos or a map pin to your removal company. It can change the vehicle choice.

## 6. Know the low-emission zones on your route

Several cities now charge vehicles that do not meet emissions standards, including London, Birmingham, Bristol, Glasgow and Edinburgh. Ask whether any charge is built into your quote and whether the vehicle will comply. Details differ by city, so check the current rules for your destination.

## 7. Give a precise inventory

The price depends on volume. List everything, including the loft, garage, shed and garden. A thorough inventory protects you from a revised price on the day and lets the company bring the right vehicle. For large homes, ask for a **video survey**.

## 8. Do a serious declutter

Every cubic foot costs money to move 200 miles. Sell, donate or recycle anything you do not truly need. It is also the cheapest time to downsize furniture that will not suit a different home.

## 9. Label for the destination

Number your boxes and label them by the **room in the new home**, not the old one. Use coloured stickers and put matching ones on the new doors. That way the crew can unload straight to the right place without you directing every box.

## 10. Pack a "journey and first-night" bag

You may arrive before or after the van. Pack a bag for yourself and your family with chargers, medication, toiletries, a change of clothes, snacks and basic bedding. Keep valuables and documents with you.

## 11. Plan the people and pets

Long moves are tiring for children and animals. If you can, arrange childcare or pet care for the loading day and plan the journey with proper breaks. Many people choose to travel after the van has left, so they are not rushing or waiting around.

## 12. Choose a company that talks to you

A good long-distance mover:

- Asks questions about access, inventory and timing before quoting
- Gives a **written fixed price** and a clear delivery window
- Explains insurance and what is covered in transit
- Has a named contact you can call on the day
- Has a plan for delays

## What does a long-distance move cost?

Distance, volume, access and dates all matter, so the sensible approach is to get a written quote. As a rough guide, a typical 2-3 bedroom move of around 200 miles tends to cost noticeably more than a local move. ## A final word on timing

The best long-distance moves are boring. They run to a plan, nobody is rushed, and the van arrives at a sensible hour. Plan early, share accurate details and give yourself a buffer, and yours can be one of them.
